How to Dissolve a Toilet Paper Clog in El Reno, OK

Toilet paper is designed to break apart in water, so a clog made only of paper often clears with patience and the right method. What does not work is repeated flushing, which fills the bowl and floods the floor.

This guide shows what to try in order, what to avoid and how to tell a simple paper clog from a main line problem.

First steps

Stop the flood and wait

If the bowl is full, do not flush again. Lift the tank lid and press the flapper down, or close the supply valve behind the toilet. Then wait. Paper clogs often loosen on their own after twenty to thirty minutes as the paper soaks.

Put towels around the base and keep the lid closed to avoid splash. Keep children and pets out of the room.

Modern toilets use 1.6 gallons or less per flush under federal rules, which is why a thick wad of paper or a wipe can stall a flush that an older toilet would have pushed through.

In order

What to try, safest first

  • ✓Pour a half cup of dish soap into the bowl and let it sit for twenty minutes to lubricate the paper.
  • ✓Add hot but not boiling water from waist height to help break down the paper. Boiling water can crack porcelain.
  • ✓Use a flange plunger with a firm seal, pushing slowly and pulling back sharply.
  • ✓Try a closet auger if the plunger fails, feeding it gently into the drain.
  • ✓Call a plumber if the water does not drop or the clog returns.
Avoid

What not to put in the bowl

Chemical drain openers are a poor choice for a toilet. They can sit in standing water and burn skin during plunging, and they can damage some pipes. They also do not dissolve a plug that includes wipes or a toy.

Wipes sold as flushable do not break down like paper, and they are among the most common causes of clogs. Keep a trash can in the bathroom for them.

When to call

Signs that it is time for a plumber

Call if the water keeps rising after you stop, if more than one fixture is affected, if you smell sewage or if you have plunged several times without success. An overflowing toilet that reaches the floor is an emergency because the water is contaminated.

A plumber can pull the toilet and clear the line from below, or use a camera to see what is blocking it. That also reveals whether the problem is a one-time clog or a sign of a damaged pipe.

Prevention

How to stop paper clogs from coming back

Most paper clogs come from using too much at once. Teach everyone in the house to flush in two batches if needed, and avoid thick or quilted paper if your toilet has a weak flush. Older low-flow toilets and toilets with long drain runs are the most sensitive.

Keep a small wastebasket by the toilet for anything that is not toilet paper. Cotton balls, cotton swabs, dental floss, feminine products and wipes do not dissolve. They catch on rough spots inside the pipe and collect paper until a plug forms. If your toilet clogs more than a couple of times a year, ask a plumber to check the flush and the line behind it.

Tools

Tools worth keeping at home

A flange plunger, which has a soft rubber extension below the cup, makes a better seal in a toilet than a flat sink plunger. A short closet auger, which has a protective sleeve to prevent scratching the porcelain, reaches clogs that a plunger cannot. Both cost little and last for years.

Keep rubber gloves, a bucket and old towels with them. If the problem is beyond these tools, it is time to call, and a plumber arriving at a cleared bathroom floor can start working faster. Our drain cleaning page explains what comes next when the clog is in the line.

Local note

If your El Reno home is on city sewer or septic

Most El Reno homes connect to the city sewer, and the City of El Reno public works office at 405-262-4070 can tell you whether a problem is in the city line. In rural parts of Canadian County, some homes use septic systems. Frequent clogs in a septic home can mean a full tank rather than a pipe clog.

If you live in Yukon or Piedmont, tell the dispatcher whether the property is on city sewer or septic. A backwater valve is required by El Reno's code on building sewers that connect to the public sewer, and a stuck valve can mimic a clog.
