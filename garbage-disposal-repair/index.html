Jammed

What to do before calling

A safe order of steps

Many jams clear in a minute. Follow this order and keep your hands out of the unit.

  • 1Turn the wall switch off and unplug the unit if there is a plug.
  • 2Find the hex slot on the bottom and turn it back and forth with the wrench that came with the unit.
  • 3Remove anything visible with tongs.
  • 4Press the red reset button on the bottom, then restore power and test with water running.

What not to grind

Habits that kill a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in volume, fibrous scraps such as celery and corn husks, and bones are the top causes of jams and clogs. Grease is the worst because it coats the pipe downstream and hardens, and the disposal does nothing to prevent that.

Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after. If clogs keep returning in the line beyond the disposal, the pipe may need hydro jetting.

A disposal that is hard-wired or plugged in must meet the National Electrical Code (NEC), and its drain connection must follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including the high loop or air gap for the dishwasher line.

Replacing

Things to decide when replacing a disposal

Horsepower

A larger motor handles tougher scraps and costs more.

Continuous or batch feed

Batch feed runs only with the stopper in place.

Mounting

Most use a common mount, but sink type matters.

Dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain ties in at the disposal inlet.

Septic note

If your property is on a septic system

Some homes outside city sewer service, in the rural parts of Canadian County, use septic tanks. A disposal adds solids to a tank and can shorten the time between pumpings. Ask your septic service about it before installing one.

Replacing

How a disposal replacement goes

1

Power off

The plumber disconnects power and the drain connections.

2

Remove

The old unit is unmounted from the sink flange and taken away.

3

Mount

The new unit is attached, connected to the drain and the dishwasher line.

4

Test

The unit is run with water, and every joint is checked for leaks.

Under the sink

The dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drains into the disposal inlet, and a plug inside the inlet must be knocked out when a disposal is first connected to a dishwasher. Skipping this step leaves the dishwasher unable to drain. If your dishwasher fills with dirty water after a disposal swap, the plug is the first thing to check.

Dishwasher drain hoses also need a high loop or an air gap to prevent dirty water from flowing back. A plumber sets this up as part of a proper install.
