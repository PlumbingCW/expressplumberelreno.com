Sewer Camera Inspection Before Buying a Home in El Reno, OK

A standard home inspection looks at what can be seen. It does not look inside the buried sewer line, which is where some of the costliest surprises hide. A sewer camera inspection fills that gap in a short visit.

This guide explains what the camera shows, how to schedule it during a sale and what to do with the result.

Why it matters

The part of the house nobody sees

The sewer lateral runs from the house to the connection with the city main. It can be clay, cast iron or plastic, and age, roots and soil movement can crack or shift it. A cracked line may drain fine today and fail after you move in.

The repair is the owner's cost, and it can be large. Knowing the condition before you close gives you room to negotiate or to walk away.

Findings

What the camera can show

Timing

When to schedule it

Book the inspection during the option period or inspection window of your contract. Tell the plumber the closing deadline so the report arrives in time. Ask the seller for access to the cleanout. If there is no cleanout, the plumber may need to pull a toilet.

Ask for the video and a written summary. A plumber should mark the location of any defect by distance from the cleanout. See sewer camera inspection for the process.

Older houses

Why age matters in El Reno

El Reno was incorporated in 1890, and homes near the Rock District and the Carnegie Library are among the oldest. Those older laterals may be clay or cast iron, and they can be decades old. A camera is a sensible step for any house built before about 1980.

Newer homes are not immune. Construction debris, a sag from poor backfill or a cracked fitting can show up on a camera in a house only a few years old.

Negotiating

What to do with the result

If the camera shows a problem, ask the seller to repair it, give a credit or reduce the price. Get a written quote for repair so the request has a number. If the line is clean, you have a baseline for the future.

If the findings are serious, such as a collapsed section, talk to your agent about your options. A repair under the house can be costly. See sewer line repair for the options and what drives price.

Beyond purchases

Other times to use a camera

Use a camera after a repeat clog, before a major remodel or before landscaping over the line. Use it after a tree is removed or when roots are suspected. A baseline video makes later comparisons easy.

If a clog is the reason you are calling, the plumber may clear the line first, then run the camera. See drain cleaning for what that involves.

What it costs

Pricing factors

The cost depends on access, the length of the line, whether cleaning is needed first and whether the plumber locates and marks the defects. A clean outdoor cleanout makes it quicker. Ask for a price in advance, and ask what is included.

The cost is small compared with the cost of a surprise repair.

Reading the report

How to make sense of what the plumber tells you

A good report lists the length of line inspected, the pipe material, any defects and where they are, and a plain recommendation. Ask the plumber to separate urgent items from watch items. A single minor root intrusion at a joint is a different story from a collapsed section.

If you are not sure how serious a finding is, ask for a second opinion with the video. A plumber who does repairs can price the work, and a second quote helps you decide how much to ask of the seller. Do not skip the written part. Verbal findings are hard to use in a negotiation.

Cleanouts

Why access matters for the inspection and for later

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives a plumber direct access to the sewer line. A house with a good outdoor cleanout is easier and cheaper to inspect and maintain. If the house has none, ask the seller about adding one before closing, or plan to have one installed after you move in.

The cleanout also tells you something about the house. A missing or buried cleanout often means no one has serviced the line in a long time. That is a reason to run the camera, not a reason to skip it. See our sewer line cleaning page for what a main line service involves.

Local note

El Reno code and records

El Reno requires building sewers to be at least 12 inches below grade and requires a backwater valve on a building sewer that connects to the public sewer. Ask during an inspection whether a valve is present and working. The City of El Reno public works at 405-262-4070 can tell you where the city main connects.

El Reno Community Development at 405-262-2000 can tell you about permits for past work. If you are buying in Yukon or Piedmont, ask that city's office. Confirm any plumber's state license on the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board lookup.
