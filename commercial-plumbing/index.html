Locally

Restaurants and shops along Route 66 and downtown

El Reno's food culture, from the Fried Onion Burger Day Festival held the first Saturday in May to the diners along U.S. Route 66, depends on kitchens that do a lot of frying. Frying means grease, and grease in a drain line is the main cause of restaurant backups.

A regular jetting schedule and a maintained grease interceptor cost less than an emergency during a busy night. Ask the City of El Reno about its rules on grease control for food service businesses.

Commercial work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by El Reno, which sets fixture counts, grease interceptor rules and backflow protection, so ask the plumber which sections apply to your occupancy.

Compliance

Backflow and code

Many commercial properties need backflow prevention on their water connections, and some require annual testing. Ask the City of El Reno and your plumber which applies to your property.

Commercial work must be done by a licensed plumber, and the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board can confirm any license.

Business types

How the work differs by type of business

Restaurants and cafes

Grease lines, floor drains, three-compartment sinks and dishwashers carry the most risk. A backup during service means closing the kitchen.

Retail and offices

Restrooms, break room sinks and water heaters dominate. A slow leak above a ceiling can damage stock and records.

Churches, schools and halls

Buildings used in bursts have long idle periods, so seals dry out and low-use drains smell. Flushing lines before an event avoids surprises.

Rental property

Landlords need a written cause for each call so repairs can be assigned correctly under a lease. Ask for notes with every visit.

Planning ahead

Preventive habits that keep a business open

Most commercial plumbing emergencies are preceded by a warning: a floor drain that smells, a restroom that flushes slowly, a kitchen sink that drains a little slower each month. Staff who are told to report those signs early save the business a closed day.

Keep the location of every shutoff valve posted near the door, label the main, and keep a list of what is on each branch line. When a plumber arrives, that list saves time while water is running. Also ask whether your insurance carrier requires documentation after a backup, because commercial policies often have their own notice deadlines.
