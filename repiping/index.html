Whole House Repiping in El Reno, OK

Repiping replaces the water supply lines throughout a house. It is the answer when leaks keep returning, water runs brown, pressure is poor everywhere, or the pipe material is known to fail. It is a planned project, and our line is answered 24/7 for anyone who needs to talk it through.

If a pipe is leaking today, see burst pipe repair first. Repiping comes after the water is under control.

Signs

Signs a house needs new pipes

  • ✓Leaks that return in different places within a year or two.
  • ✓Brown or orange water from the tap, especially after the water has sat.
  • ✓Low pressure at several fixtures even when the street pressure is fine.
  • ✓Visible corrosion, green stains or flaking at joints.
  • ✓Galvanized steel supply pipe, common in older homes, that has narrowed inside.

Local homes

What age says about the pipes in El Reno houses

El Reno was incorporated in 1890 and grew along the railroad and Route 66, so the housing stock spans more than a century. Houses from before the mid twentieth century may still carry galvanized steel, and homes built in the 1970s to 1990s may have copper or polybutylene, a gray plastic pipe that was widely installed in that era and later withdrawn.

If you do not know what you have, look where pipe enters the water heater or runs through an unfinished space. Copper is brown or green, galvanized is gray and threaded, and PEX is red, blue or white plastic. A plumber can identify what is behind the walls with an inspection.

PEX tubing should carry the ASTM F876 and F877 markings, copper tube should be marked to ASTM B88, and the installation as a whole must follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Process

How a repipe is planned and done

1

Walkthrough

The plumber counts fixtures, finds the pipe paths and checks access.

2

Quote

You get a clear price and a schedule before any wall is opened.

3

Install

New lines go in, often through attics and small wall openings.

4

Test and patch

The system is pressure tested, then openings are closed. Drywall finishing may be separate.

Living through it

What to expect at home

Water is shut off during parts of the work, so plan for a day or more without it, depending on the size of the house. Move furniture away from walls where access is needed, and protect valuables from dust.

Ask the plumber how openings will be patched, whether painting is included, and when inspections happen. Permit rules vary, so ask El Reno Community Development at 405-262-2000.
