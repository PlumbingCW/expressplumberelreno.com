Until help arrives, stop all water use, keep the toilets unflushed, and keep people away from water that has come out of a drain.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Protect the house while the line is blocked

Raw sewage is a health hazard and a fast-moving flood. Treat any backup as wastewater, not clean water, and take these steps in order.

  • 1Stop using water everywhere, including toilets, showers and the washing machine.
  • 2If wastewater is on the floor, shut off power to that area only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • 3Take photos of the damage and the affected drain for your insurance claim.
  • 4Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher which drain is overflowing and whether it is a toilet or a floor drain.

Do not run a shop vacuum on sewage. It spreads contamination and ruins the motor.

Cause and effect

Why main lines stop, and what a cleaning actually does

A main sewer line carries everything from the house to the municipal connection. Five things block it most often: roots entering a joint, grease collected over years, wipes sold as flushable, a pipe belly that holds water, and a broken section that collapses inward.

Cleaning clears the line with a drum machine or a jetter, fed through a cleanout outside the house or through a pulled toilet if there is no cleanout. A cutting head chops roots, a spinning chain scrubs the walls, and a jetter flushes what remains. A plumber who cleans the line without a camera pass leaves the cause unknown.

Checklist

Signs the problem is the main line, not one fixture

  • ✓More than one drain is slow or backing up at the same time.
  • ✓A toilet flush makes a tub or floor drain bubble.
  • ✓Sewage rises in the lowest fixture when the washing machine empties.
  • ✓A sewage smell appears near the cleanout, the yard or a floor drain.
  • ✓The problem got worse during or after heavy rain.
Local note

The backwater valve rule in El Reno

El Reno's city code adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments. One amendment requires a backwater valve on every building sewer that connects to the public sewer. The valve is a flap that lets wastewater flow out but closes if it tries to flow back in.

If your house has one, it needs occasional maintenance, because debris can hold the flap open or stick it closed. If it does not, and the house backs up when the city line is under heavy flow, a plumber can tell you whether adding a valve makes sense. Ask the El Reno Community Development office at 405-262-2000 about permit requirements for that work.
