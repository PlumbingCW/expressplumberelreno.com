Will Coconut Oil Clog Drains in El Reno, OK Homes?

Yes, coconut oil can clog drains. It is liquid when it is warm, but it solidifies at room temperature and below, so it hardens inside a cool drain pipe and collects whatever else washes by. A little may pass, but repeated use builds a coating that eventually blocks the line.

Here is why it happens, what to do instead and what to do if a clog has already formed.

The reason

Why coconut oil hardens in the pipe

Coconut oil melts at a temperature just below normal room temperature. That means it stays liquid while it is hot from the pan, then cools as it travels down the drain and sets into a waxy solid on the pipe wall. The cold water running with it hastens that.

Once a thin layer sets, soap residue, coffee grounds and food bits stick to it. The opening narrows slowly. The first sign is usually a slower drain, followed by a total stoppage on a day you have a lot of dishes.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires grease interceptors in many commercial kitchens, which tells you how seriously the code treats fats in drains.

Disposal

What to do with used coconut oil

Let it cool and solidify in the pan or a jar. Scrape it into a sealed container or a container with a lid and put it in the trash. Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing. For liquid oils, keep an empty can or jar by the stove and pour the oil in as it cools.

A garbage disposal does not solve this. It grinds food but not grease, and it pushes the fat into the pipe where it cools. See garbage disposal repair if the disposal is also giving trouble.

Home remedies

What helps and what does not

Hot water from the tap can melt a fresh grease layer near the drain opening, which is a sensible first step. It will not reach a coating several feet down. Dish soap added to hot water helps a little. Chemical cleaners are a poor choice, since they can damage pipes and make later service hazardous.

If the sink drains slowly, try a plunger or a drain stick first. Repeated slow drains mean the coating is still there, and that is the time to call.

Professional fix

How a plumber removes a grease clog

A cable machine can punch through a grease plug, but the coating remains and the clog returns. Hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high pressure water and carries the grease out. Our hydro jetting page explains when it fits and when a camera look should come first.

If a line has been clogged by grease more than once, a camera shows whether the pipe has other problems, such as a sag where grease collects.

Kitchens

Households and small food businesses

Home cooks are not the only ones who face this. Small restaurants and food trucks in town fry food and wash pans in sinks that connect to the same sewer. A grease interceptor and scheduled cleaning keep the line moving. See commercial plumbing for what a food business should plan for.

The habit is the same at any scale. Wipe pans before washing, never pour fat down a drain and schedule service before a busy season.

Beyond coconut oil

Other things that behave the same way

Coconut oil is not alone. Butter, shortening, lard, bacon grease and even melted candle wax behave the same way in a pipe. So does the grease that washes off pans with hot soapy water. The soap makes the fat travel further, and then the fat separates and sticks when the water cools.

Dairy products, rice and pasta water add starch, which gives the coating something to hold on to. Coffee grounds do not dissolve and collect on the coating. A drain that gets all of these is a drain that will eventually need a cleaning, however careful the household is.

Cleaning schedule

How often a busy kitchen needs service

A household that cooks often with fats may want a cleaning every year or two even without symptoms. A restaurant needs more regular service, often set by the volume of cooking. The point of a schedule is to remove the coating before it becomes a plug.

Ask the plumber about a camera look after a jetting visit. It shows how clean the pipe is and whether any low spot is collecting grease. See sewer camera inspection for what that involves.

Local note

Grease and the El Reno sewer

Grease clogs affect private lines and the city's own sewer mains. The City of El Reno public works office at 405-262-4070 can answer questions about reporting a problem in the public line, and the city's code addresses how building sewers connect to the public sewer. A backwater valve is required on a building sewer that connects to it, and a grease-clogged line can make that valve hold open.

If you live in Yukon or Piedmont, the same habits apply, and your city has its own contacts.
