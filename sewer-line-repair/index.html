Diagnosis first

How a plumber finds what is wrong with a sewer line

A cleaning clears a line. A repair fixes a line that is physically damaged. The difference matters because paying for the second cleaning in a year, without a camera, is the most common way homeowners overspend on a broken pipe.

The plumber runs a camera through the line and watches for roots at joints, a belly that holds water, a crack along the pipe, an offset joint, or a collapsed section. The footage shows the distance from the cleanout to the problem, which tells the plumber where to dig or where to line.

Local code

Depth, valves and permits under El Reno code

El Reno's plumbing code amends the International Plumbing Code on two points that touch sewer work. Building sewers must sit at least 12 inches below grade, and a backwater valve is required on a building sewer that connects to the public sewer. A good repair plan accounts for both, especially when the new section is shallower than the old one.

The same code requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed plumber, with listed exemptions that can apply to small repairs and to owners working on their own homes. Rules vary, so ask the Community Development license and permits desk at 405-262-2000 whether your repair needs a permit, then confirm any plumber's state license on the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board lookup.

Causes

What damages sewer lines in this part of Oklahoma

El Reno sits near the I-40 and U.S. 81 interchange, in a part of Canadian County with hot summers and occasional hard freezes. Those conditions, plus age and trees, shape the failure modes.

Roots

Roots enter at joints and grow into the pipe wall, widening cracks with each season.

Aging pipe

Clay, cast iron and early plastic pipes crack or deform over decades.

Ground movement

Heavy rain and dry spells shift soil, which can offset joints or bend the pipe.

Construction damage

Digging near the line, a fence post or a tree removal can crack the pipe.
