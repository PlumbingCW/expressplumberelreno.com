Overflow

What to do about a toilet that is overflowing right now

Stop the flood in four steps

Do not flush again. Each flush adds water to a bowl that cannot drain.

  • 1Turn the oval handle on the supply line behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  • 2Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down to stop water entering the bowl.
  • 3Lay towels around the base and keep children and pets out of the room.
  • 4Call the 24/7 line and say whether other fixtures are also slow.

Why it matters

A leaking toilet base can rot the floor

A slow leak at the base seeps into the subfloor, where it softens the wood and grows mold out of sight. If the floor around the toilet feels soft, or the toilet rocks, do not wait. The wax ring is cheap, but the floor beneath it is not.

A leak that appears only when you flush points to the wax seal or the closet flange. A leak that appears all the time from the tank points to the supply line or the tank bolts.

Toilets sold in the United States are limited to 1.6 gallons per flush, WaterSense labeled models use 1.28 or less, and installation must follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including a secure flange connection.

Choosing a new toilet

Gravity flush

Reliable and simple. Most homes use them.

Pressure assist

Stronger flush, louder, common in commercial settings.

Elongated bowl

More comfortable. Needs a little extra space.

Comfort height

Water use

High efficiency and the water bill

Federal rules cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, and many models use 1.28 or less. Replacing a very old toilet can cut water use noticeably, so a running or leaking one can show up on the El Reno utility bill. Ask the City at 405-262-4070 about your usage history if the bill is unusually high.
