El Reno plumbing, locally

What El Reno houses ask of their plumbing

El Reno was incorporated in 1890 and grew around the railroad, Fort Reno and later Route 66. The blocks around the Rock District and the Carnegie Library hold some of the oldest buildings in Canadian County. Older sewer laterals in neighborhoods like that can be clay or cast iron, and roots find the joints, so a drain that keeps returning deserves a camera look. Start with drain cleaning or a sewer camera inspection.

Newer houses on the edges of town and in Yukon and Piedmont use PEX and PVC, so the common problems are different: water heaters in tight closets, hose bibs that freeze, and construction debris in drains. A leaking tank needs water heater replacement, and a frozen line that has split needs burst pipe repair.

Weather is the other local factor. Central Oklahoma can swing from a warm week to a hard freeze, and the National Weather Service in Norman issues the warnings. Insulate exposed lines before a freeze and know where your main shutoff is.

El Reno also has its own code. The city adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments, requires a backwater valve on a building sewer that connects to the public sewer, and expects contractors to be state licensed and registered with the city. Call El Reno Community Development at 405-262-2000 about permits.
