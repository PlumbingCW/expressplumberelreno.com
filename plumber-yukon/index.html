Housing eras

What the age of a Yukon house tells a plumber

Yukon was platted in 1891 and incorporated in 1901, but most of the houses you see today came later. The population climbed from about 3,000 in 1960 to more than 8,000 in 1970 and more than 17,000 by 1980, and it passed 23,000 in the 2020 census. A large share of Yukon homes therefore date from the 1970s through the 1990s.

That matters in the walls. Houses from those decades often have copper supply lines that are now old enough to pit, original water heaters on their second or third replacement, and, in some, polybutylene supply pipe, a gray plastic that was widely installed then and later fell out of favor. If your house is from that era and you have had two leaks in a few years, ask about a repiping review instead of another patch.

Plumbing in Yukon homes is governed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as that city adopts it, which is why a repair in a 1980s house may need to meet today's code even though the original did not.

Services in Yukon

The calls we take most in Yukon

Clogged lines come first. A cable can clear a stoppage, but a line that returns every few months deserves drain cleaning with a camera look or hydro jetting. Next are water heaters. A tank with a leaking body needs replacement, and one that has simply gone cold often needs only a repair.

Slab leaks are the third common call in homes of this age. A warm patch on the floor or a meter that moves with everything off points that way, and leak detection confirms it before anyone cuts concrete.

Local note

Yukon events, freezes and your pipes

Yukon holds the Chisholm Trail and Crawfish Festival on the first Saturday of June and the Czech Festival on the first Saturday of October, and the Czech Hall on Route 66 is a state historic site. Festival weekends bring houseguests, and houseguests mean extra showers, extra flushes and extra strain on an older drain line. If your drains run slow, have them checked before the crowd arrives.

Oklahoma weather is the other pattern. When a hard freeze follows a mild week, exposed pipes in garages and crawlspaces burst. The National Weather Service in Norman issues the warnings, and insulating exposed lines costs far less than burst pipe repair.
