Tankless Water Heater Install and Repair in El Reno, OK

Tankless water heaters heat water only when you open a tap, so they do not hold a tank of hot water. They save space and can supply hot water without running out, but they put more demand on gas lines, venting and water quality.

How it works

What changes when you go tankless

A tankless unit uses a burner or elements to heat water as it flows through a heat exchanger. Because it only works when water is running, there is no standby tank to keep warm, and the unit mounts on a wall.

The trade-off is demand. A gas tankless unit commonly needs a larger gas line than a tank unit and a vent rated for its type. An electric unit can draw a large amount of current and may need a heavy circuit. A plumber confirms both before you buy.

Gas tankless installations follow the International Fuel Gas Code and the manufacturer's listed vent instructions, while electric units follow the National Electrical Code (NEC) for circuit and breaker sizing.

Honest view

Tankless versus tank

Tank heater

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simple, familiar repairs
  • Runs out when the tank is empty
  • Uses floor space

Tankless heater

  • Hot water as long as the flow is within capacity
  • Wall mounted, saves floor space
  • Needs sized gas and venting
  • Needs descaling in hard water

Neither is always better. A large family with frequent back-to-back showers tends to benefit from tankless, while a small household may be happier with a tank and a lower bill for the unit.

Scale

Flushing and scale in tankless units

The narrow passages inside a tankless heat exchanger collect mineral scale from hard water. A scaled exchanger runs hotter, delivers less flow and may throw an error code. Flushing with a descaling solution, usually once a year, clears it.

If you do not know your water hardness, a hardness test strip answers it, and the City of El Reno can tell you about its supply. Pairing a tankless unit with a water softener reduces how often it needs flushing.

Safety

Venting and gas safety

Call before you restart a unit that shut itself off

A tankless unit that shuts down is often protecting itself.

  • 1Gas units must vent exhaust outdoors through the correct vent material.
  • 2A blocked or disconnected vent can let carbon monoxide into the house.
  • 3Install carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas.
  • 4If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility from outside.
