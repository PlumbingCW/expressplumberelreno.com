What to do first: confirm a leak in five minutes

The meter test

Before anyone arrives, you can check whether water is moving through the house when it should not be.

  • 1Turn off every tap, the ice maker and the irrigation system, and stop the washer and dishwasher.
  • 2Find your water meter, usually in a box near the street, and lift the lid carefully.
  • 3Look at the small leak indicator, a dial or triangle that spins when any water moves.
  • 4If it keeps moving with everything off, water is leaking somewhere after the meter.
  • 5Write down the reading, wait 30 minutes and check again.

Signs

Quiet signs of a leak in El Reno homes

Hidden leaks show themselves slowly. A musty smell in a closet, a stain that grows after rain or without rain, warped flooring, peeling paint, or a patch of lawn that stays green and soggy all summer are all worth checking.

Bills help too. If your utility statement is high and your habits have not changed, the leak could be in the house, in the service line between the meter and the house, or in an irrigation system. Ask the City of El Reno utility billing office at 405-262-4070 about your usage history, then compare it with the meter test.

Pressure testing of supply lines follows the test methods in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which is why a plumber will hold a set test pressure for a set time instead of guessing from the meter alone.

Where leaks hide

The places leaks usually start

Toilet tanks

A flapper that does not seal leaks quietly into the bowl and can waste a great deal of water.

Under slabs

Copper lines in older slab homes can pit and pinhole. See slab leak repair.

Service line

The pipe from the meter to the house can fail underground. See water line repair.

Irrigation and hose bibs

A sprinkler valve stuck open or a failed outdoor faucet can run all day unnoticed.

Insurance

Documenting a leak for a claim

Homeowner policies vary. Many cover sudden and accidental water damage but not long-term seepage or the cost of finding the leak. Ask your agent which applies, and take photos and video before anything is dried or removed.

A written note from the plumber that names where the leak was and what caused it helps an adjuster. Ask for it when you book.
