Backflow Testing and Repair in El Reno, OK

A backflow preventer stops dirty water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Lawn irrigation systems, fire sprinkler lines, commercial kitchens and some home setups depend on one, and the device has to work every time pressure changes.

Testing is planned work, but a failed assembly that leaks or sprays is not. Our line is answered 24/7, and a licensed plumber can usually schedule the same day when one is free.

What it is

What a backflow preventer protects against

Water normally moves from the city main to your taps. If pressure in the main drops, because of a break or heavy fire flow, water in your pipes can be pulled backward. Without protection, anything connected to your plumbing can be drawn into the supply: fertilizer in an irrigation line, boiler chemicals, or dirty water from a hose left in a bucket.

A backflow assembly uses check valves or an air gap to make that impossible. Because the parts are springs and rubber seals, they wear, and a device that worked last year may not hold today.

Backflow assemblies are made to published standards, such as ASSE 1013 for reduced pressure assemblies, ASSE 1015 for double check assemblies and ASSE 1020 for pressure vacuum breakers, and the plumbing code (IPC) that El Reno adopts requires protection wherever a cross connection exists.

Failure

What to do when a backflow assembly is leaking or spraying

Steps to take

A reduced pressure assembly is designed to dump some water when it senses a problem. That is a sign it is working, but a continuous discharge means something has failed.

  • 1Close the shutoff valve upstream of the assembly if you can reach it safely.
  • 2Keep the area clear of electrical equipment, since the discharge can be heavy.
  • 3Do not drink or use water from a line whose assembly you think has failed until it is checked.
  • 4Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Local rules

Who requires testing and who may do it

Requirements come from the water supplier and the state, and they vary by the type of hazard. Many utilities require a test when a device is installed and on a regular schedule afterward, and they require the tester to hold a certification. Ask the City of El Reno at 405-262-4070 whether your property needs a test and what paperwork to file.

Do not assume a plumber can pull a permit or file a report in every case. Ask whoever you hire whether they hold the testing certification the city expects, and keep the test report with your property records.
