Leaky Sink Faucet Repair in El Reno, OK

A leaky sink faucet is one of the most common plumbing complaints, and one of the most fixable. The drip comes from a worn part, and the part depends on the type of faucet and where the water appears. A repair kit often solves it in under an hour.

This guide helps you find the leak, decide what you can handle and know when to call. If water is spraying or flooding the cabinet, close the shutoff valves under the sink and call the 24/7 line.

Where is it leaking

Locate the drip before you buy parts

Dry the faucet and the sink deck with a towel, then run the water and watch. A drip from the spout when the faucet is off points to a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Water at the base of the handle points to a worn O-ring or loose packing nut. Water at the base of the faucet where it meets the sink points to loose mounting or a failed gasket.

Also look under the sink. A wet cabinet floor may come from the supply lines, the shutoff valves or the drain, not the faucet. Wipe each connection dry and watch where the first bead forms.

Replacement faucets should meet the federal flow limit and, ideally, carry the EPA WaterSense label, and under-sink work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Basic repair

The usual steps for a cartridge or washer repair

1

Shut off the water

Close the valves under the sink and open the faucet to release pressure.

2

Remove the handle

Pry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.

3

Replace the part

Pull out the cartridge or washer and match it with a new one.

4

Reassemble and test

Reassemble, open the valves slowly and check for drips.

Parts

Finding the right replacement

Bring the old cartridge or washer to a hardware store. Match it by size and shape, and look for a brand name or model number stamped on the faucet. Photograph the faucet before you take it apart so you can reassemble it correctly.

If the faucet is old and the parts are hard to find, replacing it is often smarter than hunting. A new faucet with a good warranty also gives you a cleaner finish and better flow.

Stuck valves

The shutoff valve problem

The valves under the sink often seize if they have not been turned in years. Forcing a stuck valve can snap its stem and flood the cabinet. If a valve will not turn fully, or leaks when you do turn it, stop and call. Replacing the valve is a small job for a plumber, and it keeps future repairs simple.

Replace braided supply lines at the same time if they are old. They can fail without warning, and the cost is small compared with the cost of water damage.

Repair or replace

How to decide

Repair when the faucet is in good shape and the parts are easy to find. Replace when the finish is worn, when it leaks again soon after a repair or when you are already remodeling. A faucet that leaks at the base or the body, not at a seal, usually needs replacing.

Our faucet and sink repair page covers what a plumber checks. A kitchen faucet with a disposal and a dishwasher connection can have extra leak points under the sink. See garbage disposal repair if the water appears below the unit.

Cost of waiting

Why not to ignore a drip

A faucet that drips once a second wastes a surprising amount of water over a month, and it shows on your bill. More important, a slow leak under the sink soaks the cabinet floor, swells the particleboard and grows mold. By the time you notice the smell, the cabinet is often ruined.

Put a small container under the trap and check it weekly if you cannot fix the leak right away. That tells you whether it is getting worse.

Local note

Water quality and faucet wear in El Reno

Minerals in water wear faucet parts and clog aerators. The City of El Reno utility office at 405-262-4070 can share its water quality report, and a hardness test strip gives a quick number. If you see white crust at the aerator, soak it in vinegar and rinse.

The plumbing code that El Reno adopts also requires fixtures to meet water use limits, so a new faucet should carry a flow rating. Homes in Yukon and Piedmont follow their own city offices. For a leak in the supply to the faucet, ask whether a licensed plumber should handle it, and confirm the license on the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board lookup.
