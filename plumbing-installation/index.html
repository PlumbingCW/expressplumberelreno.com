Scope

What plumbing installation covers

Installation includes supply lines, drain and vent piping, shutoff valves, fixtures such as sinks, tubs, showers and toilets, appliance hookups such as washers, dishwashers and ice makers, and gas lines for appliances where permitted.

The plumber checks that the new work ties into the existing system correctly, that drains slope enough to flow, and that vents let the system breathe. A drain without a proper vent gurgles and can siphon its trap dry, which lets sewer gas into the house.

New drain, waste and vent work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and gas lines for appliances follow the International Fuel Gas Code, so ask which edition the city enforces.

Rules

Permits and licensing in El Reno

El Reno requires plumbing work by contractors to be done by a state-licensed plumber registered with the city, with listed exemptions. The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board licenses plumbers, and its lookup tool confirms a license. The Oklahoma Uniform Building Code Commission adopts the state plumbing code that the city's local amendments build on.

Ask El Reno Community Development at 405-262-2000 how permits and inspections apply to your project. Rough-in inspections happen before walls close, so a plumber who skips that step creates a problem you will find later.

Slab or crawlspace

How the foundation changes the job

Slab homes hide drain lines in concrete, so adding a bathroom may mean cutting the floor to reach them. Crawlspace and pier-and-beam homes are easier, because pipes run below the floor where a plumber can reach them, though freezing becomes a concern in an unconditioned space.

Whatever the foundation, ask where the drain will tie in and how far it runs. Long horizontal runs need enough slope to carry waste, and a plumber who has seen the actual site can tell you whether the plan works before the money is spent.

Fixture notes

Choosing fixtures that last

  • ✓Pick fixtures with replacement parts that are easy to find.
  • ✓Check rough-in dimensions before buying a toilet, vanity or tub.
  • ✓Install shutoff valves at every fixture so one repair does not close the house.
  • ✓Use supply lines rated for the pressure at your home.
  • ✓Keep the manuals and model numbers in one folder for later repairs.
