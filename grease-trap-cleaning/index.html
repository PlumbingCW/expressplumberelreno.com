Grease Trap Cleaning in El Reno, OK

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the sewer. When it fills, the kitchen drains slow, odors rise and, eventually, the line backs up into a prep sink or a floor drain on a Friday night.

Our line is open 24/7, and a plumber can usually be scheduled the same day when a crew is free. If a kitchen drain is backing up now, stop the dishwasher and the sinks and call.

How it works

What a grease trap does and why it fills

A trap slows wastewater so grease floats to the top and solids sink to the bottom. Cleaner water flows out the middle. The trap does not destroy grease. It stores it, and when the storage is full, the grease passes through and coats the line beyond.

Kitchens that fry food, wash greasy pans and run dishwashers all day fill traps faster. The schedule that works depends on the volume of cooking and the size of the unit, not on the calendar alone.

Grease interceptors are sized and installed under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and plumbing and drainage institute standards, and cleaning logs should be kept the way the City of El Reno asks.

What to do when the kitchen is already backing up

Protect the kitchen and the customers

Wastewater with grease is slippery and unsanitary, so treat a backup as a food safety problem.

  • 1Stop dishwashing and close the affected sinks.
  • 2Move food and equipment away from floor drains.
  • 3Block off the wet area so no one slips.
  • 4Call the 24/7 line and say what is backing up.

Locally

El Reno kitchens and the sewer

El Reno's food scene, from the diners on Route 66 to the Fried Onion Burger Day Festival on the first Saturday in May, runs on griddles and fryers. That volume of grease has to go somewhere, and the city's sewer mains are shared by every business on the street. A trap that is cleaned on time protects your kitchen and the public line.

Ask the City of El Reno at 405-262-4070 about requirements for food service grease control, including whether an interceptor is required and what records the city expects. Keep cleaning logs, since an inspector may ask.

Kitchen habits

Habits that make a trap last

  • ✓Scrape plates and pans into the trash before washing.
  • ✓Never pour fryer oil down any drain. Use a sealed container for pickup.
  • ✓Use strainers in every sink and empty them.
  • ✓Keep a written cleaning log with dates.
  • ✓Train every shift on what goes in the drain.
