The neighborhood

What Crimson Lake Estates is

Crimson Lake Estates is a new-construction residential community in El Reno, Oklahoma, in ZIP 73036. Builder pages place it off Interstate 40 and describe it as steps from Lake El Reno. Two national builders, LGI Homes and D.R. Horton, are selling homes there, and a golf course industry report described the project as planned near the Crimson Creek golf course.

The builder listings show three to five bedroom homes, one and two story plans, from roughly 1,150 to about 2,480 square feet. Current listings show homes still under construction in 2026, so some owners are new and some streets are still being built.

That is a residential subdivision, not a commercial district, and it is part of El Reno rather than a neighboring town. For the wider picture of where we work, see the service area page and our main El Reno plumbing page.

Owning a new build

Plumbing questions that come up in newer homes

These are general points for any recently built house. We have not inspected homes in this subdivision, and nothing here describes the pipe material or fixtures your builder used.

Check the builder warranty first

A new home usually comes with a warranty from the builder. Read it before you pay for a repair, and photograph the problem. If water is flooding the house, stop the water first and sort out who pays afterward.

Find your shutoffs on day one

Locate the main water valve, the shutoff at the water heater and the valves under each sink and toilet. Knowing where they are turns a flood into a small mess.

Watch the first drains and fixtures

Construction debris can end up in new drain lines, and a fitting that was not fully tightened can weep for months. A slow drain or a damp cabinet in the first year is worth a look.

Plan for the first freeze

Oklahoma weather can swing from mild to hard freeze quickly. Disconnect hoses from outdoor faucets and ask your builder how the exterior lines are protected.

Outdoors

Yards, hoses and the lake

Living near a lake means more time outside, and outdoor plumbing gets more use. Hose bibs, sprinkler connections and any outdoor sink are the first things to freeze or crack. Drain and disconnect before a freeze, and look for drips at the spigot once the weather warms.

A wet spot in the yard that stays after the rain stops can mean a leak in a service line. Check the meter before you assume it is groundwater, and call if the meter keeps moving.
