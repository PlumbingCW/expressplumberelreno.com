Emergency Slab Leak Repair in El Reno, OK

A slab leak is a break in a pressurized water line that runs in or under the concrete foundation. It can cause hot spots on the floor, a constant hiss, and a bill that climbs for weeks. Signs

Symptoms of a slab leak

Call if you notice these

A slab leak rarely looks like a puddle. The signs are quieter.

  • 1A warm patch on a tile or wood floor, which points to a hot water line.
  • 2The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • 3Water pressure that drops without explanation.
  • 4Damp carpet, lifting flooring or cracks in a wall near the slab.
  • 5A meter that moves with the house shut off.
Cause

Why lines under a slab fail

Copper lines can pit from the inside as chemicals in the water wear at the pipe, and can rub against concrete or gravel as the slab shifts through wet and dry seasons. Hot water lines expand and contract, which stresses joints and fittings.

In central Oklahoma, clay soils swell when wet and shrink when dry, and that movement can stress a foundation over years. The plumber will not guess the cause from the surface, which is why locating the leak comes first, using leak detection tools.

Pipe replaced under or through a slab must meet the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including sleeving where a line passes through concrete, and the new pipe should carry its ASTM marking.

Local housing

Slab foundations across El Reno, Yukon and Piedmont

Slab-on-grade foundations are common in Oklahoma, and the neighborhoods that grew in the 1970s and 1980s often have copper supply lines poured into or under the slab. Newer subdivisions use PEX, which resists pitting but can still be damaged during construction.

If your house is older and you have had one slab leak, ask the plumber whether a second is likely. A pressure test across the system and a look at the pipe material help you choose between another spot repair and a reroute.

Insurance

Documentation and coverage

Coverage differs by policy. Many pay for the damage a sudden leak causes to your home but not always for accessing the pipe. Ask your agent before work starts, and ask the plumber for a written note naming the leak and its location.
