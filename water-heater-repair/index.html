Safety

What to do about a gas smell, scorching or a hot relief valve

When to stop and call right away

Most water heater problems are inconvenient. A few are dangerous.

  • 1Smell of gas: leave, then call the gas utility and us from outside.
  • 2Water spraying from the relief valve or its drain pipe: shut the cold supply and the fuel.
  • 3Scorch marks, soot or a yellow flame on a gas unit: turn it off.
  • 4Water around an electric unit: leave the breaker off until a plumber checks it.

Winter

Why heaters fail in cold weather

Oklahoma winters are uneven. A warm week can end with a hard freeze, and the Oklahoma Mesonet and the National Weather Service in Norman issue the warnings. Cold incoming water makes the heater work harder, and a garage or an unheated closet adds to the load.

Before a freeze, insulate exposed hot and cold lines and make sure a heater in a garage is not sitting next to a draft. If a pipe has already frozen, the problem is no longer the heater alone, and burst pipe repair may be the right call.

Repairs to a gas unit follow the International Fuel Gas Code and the manufacturer's instructions, and a plumber should check the relief valve and discharge pipe against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) while on site.

Maintenance

Maintenance that prevents repeat repairs

Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, test the relief valve by lifting its lever briefly, and look for rust at the base. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that protects the tank lining from corrosion, and checking it every few years can extend the tank's life.

If the repair bill is approaching the price of a new tank on an old unit, ask the plumber to compare both. Sometimes the honest answer is replacement.
