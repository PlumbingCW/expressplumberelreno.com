The short version

What hydro jetting does that a snake cannot

Hydro jetting sends water through a flexible hose at very high pressure and out of a nozzle that fires backward and sideways. The jets strip grease, soap scum, scale and fine roots off the inside of the pipe. A cable, by comparison, bores a hole through the blockage and leaves the coating behind.

That difference explains the pattern many callers describe: the drain was snaked, ran freely for a few weeks, then clogged again at the same spot. The pipe was never actually clean. Jetting restores the full diameter, and it can carry debris all the way to the municipal connection instead of pushing it a few feet downstream.

Jetting should be done within the pipe manufacturer's pressure limits, and any pipe repair that follows must meet the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in El Reno.

Backup first

When a backed-up main needs jetting right away

Why a sewage backup is not a wait-until-morning problem

A main line backup sends wastewater into the lowest drain in the house, usually a tub, shower or floor drain. That water carries bacteria, and it soaks flooring quickly. If a cable could not clear the line, or the line clogs under the same conditions every time it rains or the laundry runs, jetting is often the next step.

  • 1Stop all water use, including toilets, until the line is cleared.
  • 2Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the affected drain.
  • 3Ask the plumber to run a camera before and after, so you can see the result.
Symptoms

Signs the pipe is coated rather than blocked

Clogs return in weeks

A line that clears and then fails again in the same place almost always has buildup left on the walls.

Slow drains in every bathroom

A coating thick enough to reduce the whole line's capacity slows every fixture gradually.

Gurgling after cable service

Air still struggling past scale in the pipe is a sign the cable did not restore full flow.

Greasy smell from the sink

Kitchen lines in busy homes and small restaurants collect fats that harden as they cool.

Trade-offs

Cable versus jetter: an honest comparison

Cable machine

  • Quick and low cost for a one-time clog
  • Works through a small cleanout
  • Leaves grease and scale on the pipe wall
  • Roots return unless cut at the joint

Hydro jetter

  • Removes buildup from the pipe wall
  • Handles grease, scale and many root masses
  • Often a longer visit with more equipment
  • Not for collapsed or badly broken pipe

A plumber should look at the line first. Jetting a cracked clay pipe can push water into the surrounding soil, which is why a camera check often comes before the jet.

Where it fits locally

Jetting for restaurants, shops and older homes in El Reno

Kitchens along the downtown corridor see heavy grease, and the city's annual Fried Onion Burger Day Festival, held the first Saturday in May since 1988, is a reminder of how much frying happens in this town. A restaurant drain that handles that volume benefits from scheduled jetting, ahead of a rush rather than during one. Business owners can read more on our commercial plumbing page.

Homes are the other half of the work. In older neighborhoods the lines can be clay, cast iron or an early plastic, and roots or scale narrow them over decades. Jetting is a fair option there as long as a camera confirms the pipe is intact.
