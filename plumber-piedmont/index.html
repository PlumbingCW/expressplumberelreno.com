What to do when water is already loose

Four steps for a Piedmont home

Newer houses move water fast, because supply lines are under steady pressure and a split fitting can empty a lot of water in an hour.

  • 1Turn off the main valve, usually in the garage, a utility closet or at the meter.
  • 2Open the lowest hose bib to drain the lines.
  • 3Switch off power to wet rooms if you can reach the panel safely.
  • 4Call the 24/7 line and tell us the street, and whether you have city sewer.
A growing town

Why Piedmont plumbing problems differ from older towns

Piedmont began as a railroad market town in 1903, stayed small for decades, and grew as a bedroom community for Oklahoma City workers from the 1970s onward. The 2020 census counted 7,402 people, up nearly 30 percent from 2010, and much of the housing is new.

Newer homes have PEX and PVC, so you are less likely to see corroded copper and more likely to meet construction-era problems: a loose fitting, a missed vent, a water heater installed in a tight closet, or debris left in a drain line. Those are fixable, and they are often covered by a builder warranty, so check before you pay.

Plumbing in Piedmont homes is governed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as that city adopts it, and new subdivisions are normally built to the version in force when they were permitted.

Storm season

Tornado season, shelters and your plumbing

Piedmont remembers May 24, 2011, when an EF5 tornado destroyed about 180 homes in the area. Many of the houses built since were designed with storm safety in mind. Storm season brings power outages, and a power outage means sump pumps, if you have one, stop working while heavy rain falls.

After a storm, check for water at the lowest drains, test the water heater for a pilot that went out, and look at the main valve if a tree or debris has hit the house. A sewage backup after a storm is a reason to call right away. See sewer line cleaning.
